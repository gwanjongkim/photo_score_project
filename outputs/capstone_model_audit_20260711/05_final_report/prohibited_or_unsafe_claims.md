# Prohibited or Unsafe Claims

## Controlled evidence levels

Evidence level always applies to the **original claim**, not to its replacement wording.

- `DIRECTLY_PROVEN` — the exact bounded claim is established by executed tests, source, bytes, or raw rows. It may still require context so it is not misused as a broader claim.
- `STRONGLY_SUPPORTED` — multiple independent evidence lines agree, with no material contradiction, but the claim is not a complete end-to-end proof.
- `STRONG_BUT_INCOMPLETE` — substantial evidence exists, but at least one necessary protocol, lineage, or runtime link is missing. It must never be presented as fully proven.
- `WEAKLY_SUPPORTED` — only limited, indirect, small-sample, or identity-incomplete evidence exists.
- `UNSUPPORTED` — the claim is asserted without evidence capable of establishing it, or draws an invalid inference from real evidence.
- `REFUTED` — direct evidence contradicts the claim.
- `UNVERIFIED` — the required external or historical evidence was unavailable, so truth cannot be determined.

“May be used with qualification” applies to the original claim. `NO` means the original wording must not be used; only the replacement wording is permitted.

## Absolute claim boundary

Do not use “official,” “reproduced,” “production,” “deployed,” “mobile-ready,” “paper-faithful,” or “state of the art” unless the sentence names the exact artifact, data split, metric protocol, and runtime boundary that supports the term. No state-of-the-art comparison was established by this audit.
